THE LINE · THE FULL STORY

One career, nine stops.

AS TOLD BY AMEY JOSHI

Kodoli

THE 2000s

Every line starts somewhere small. Mine starts in Kodoli, a town near Kolhapur, in front of a computer I didn't yet understand. Games got me first. Then the machines running them. Weekends meant LAN cables across the floor and Counter-Strike and Dota tournaments — and a question that wouldn't leave me alone: how does all of this actually work?

Nobody called it a career. It was curiosity with a keyboard.

Bharati Vidyapeeth

BCA YEARS

College gave the curiosity a partner — my co-founder, still one of the best programmers I've ever worked with. We built project after project, trading skills like cards. Our final-year project refused to stay a project: an Office Automation System our college actually deployed. Real people, depending on something we built.

We didn't want jobs. We wanted a company.

Zenox Technologies

2018–19 → 2024 · KOLHAPUR

Right after graduation we rented a small apartment, called it an office, and registered Zenox Technologies. Year one: no clients. So we built anyway — websites, plugins, browser extensions, tools nobody had asked for — until word of mouth did what advertising couldn't. One client became two. Two became ten.

Our rule: never say "we can't." Learn it first. Then build it.

Red signal — 2020

HELD BETWEEN STATIONS

Then COVID. Our clients were small businesses — the projects vanished overnight. We cut costs, held on, survived. And when the first lockdown lifted, we bet on the thing we loved most. Gaming.

RB Esports

FEB 2021 · KOLHAPUR · STILL RUNNING

Our own gaming café — everything I knew about hardware, networks, and players, poured into one room. We built what we believed was the best gaming café in the city. Packed from day one.

Then the doors closed again. Five months, on borrowed money. Quitting would have been easier. We paid, we waited, we reopened — and came back bigger: PCs, laptops, hardware. And a Zenox that had leveled up to university portals and serious platforms.

Two red signals in two years. The train held both times.

The Interchange

LATE 2023 · CHANGE HERE FOR THE BANKING LINE

By the end of 2023 the business was stable — and I'd hit the ceiling of what Kolhapur could offer me. Looking back across every project, a pattern stared back: I was always the one asking why. The one turning scattered ideas into structured products. The one standing between the business problem and the technical answer.

There's a name for that job. Discovering Business Analysis felt less like changing careers and more like finally putting a name to what I'd been doing all along.

I changed lines.

IDFC FIRST Bank

2024 · MUMBAI

Banking was another world. Here, software isn't built and shipped — it passes through business, compliance, security, audit, operations, vendors, and regulators before a customer ever touches it. On NRI-PIS, ASBA, Demat, and API integrations, I learned that precision beats speed — and that documentation is a form of respect.

Consulting taught me to build. Banking taught me to build responsibly.

Fino Payments Bank

2025 → · NAVI MUMBAI

At Fino, I learned what national scale feels like. The defining stretch: migrating the bank's core from FIS to Finacle — the closest thing technology has to replacing the tracks under a moving train. Months of planning compressed into a handful of execution windows. Hundreds of people — business, engineering, QA, infrastructure, vendors, leadership — moving in sync, solving problems in real time. It was exhausting. I loved every bit of it.

Great systems aren't built by brilliant individuals. They're built by teams that trust each other under pressure.

After that: AePS, DMT, eKYC, mATM certification, 3DS migration — the systems millions of people quietly depend on, every single day.

Next stop —

THE BOARD IS STILL FLIPPING

I'm not chasing a designation. I'm chasing scale. Zenox taught me to build from nothing. Banking taught me to build at scale. The plan is to keep both — startup speed, enterprise thinking — and one day, products that outlive me.

This website is one station along the way. The train is still moving.
