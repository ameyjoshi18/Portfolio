Upstream

One ask. Five streams before it’s a river.

An example: letting a customer withdraw cash with a fingerprint at an agent’s shop. What someone says, and what it has to become.

  1. 1Intent

    “Customers should be able to take out cash with just their fingerprint.”

    One sentence everyone agrees on. Who the customer is, what they get, and what counts as done.

  2. 2Constraints

    “Should be simple. The device is already there.”

    The fingerprint has to be verified by UIDAI. The agent’s device, the acquiring bank, NPCI and the customer’s own bank each have their own rules and time-outs. Every boundary gets mapped first.

  3. 3Specification

    “Just tell the developers what to build.”

    Who sends what to whom, in what order, and what happens when a leg fails. Written so business, engineering and the vendor all read it the same way.

  4. 4Acceptance

    “It worked in the demo.”

    Test cases for the clean path and the ugly ones: a fingerprint that doesn’t match, a time-out after the account is debited, the same request sent twice.

  5. 5Production

    “So we’re live on Friday?”

    Sign-offs, a release note, and a way back if it goes wrong. Then watching the first real transactions go through.

Three channels

The customer sees a result. The river runs through every leg.

What the customer sees

  1. Hands cash to the agent.
  2. Gives the account number back home.
  3. Gets a confirmation. The family gets the money.
  1. Gives an Aadhaar number and a bank name.
  2. Puts a finger on the scanner.
  3. Takes the cash and a receipt.
  1. Hands over a debit card.
  2. Types a PIN.
  3. Takes the cash and a receipt.

What the system sees

  1. Agent app takes the sender, the beneficiary and the amount.
  2. Checks on the sender’s KYC and transfer limits.
  3. Cash-in is booked against the agent at the bank.
  4. IMPS request leaves the bank for NPCI.
  5. NPCI switches it to the beneficiary’s bank.
  6. Beneficiary bank credits the account and answers.
  7. Confirmation travels back to the agent and the customer.
  8. Settlement and reconciliation close it out, including the ones that timed out.
  1. Agent’s device captures Aadhaar number, bank, amount and the fingerprint.
  2. Acquiring bank’s switch builds the request.
  3. NPCI receives it and routes it.
  4. UIDAI authenticates the fingerprint.
  5. Customer’s bank checks the balance and debits the account.
  6. Response returns along the same path.
  7. Agent pays out the cash.
  8. Settlement between the banks, and a reversal when a response is lost after the debit.
  1. Micro-ATM reads the card and takes the PIN, encrypted at the device.
  2. Acquiring bank’s switch builds the request.
  3. NPCI routes it over the ATM network to the card’s bank.
  4. Issuing bank checks the card and PIN and debits the account.
  5. Response returns along the same path.
  6. Agent pays out the cash.
  7. Settlement between the banks. No UIDAI leg on this one.

The bridge

At Fino Payments Bank I worked on moving the bank’s core from FIS to Finacle. No maintenance window is big enough to take a live bank offline, so the bank kept running while its core was changed underneath it. Five spans had to be ready.
